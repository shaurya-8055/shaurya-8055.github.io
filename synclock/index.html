The editor

Fewer taps than a sticky note

Type one line and press enter. That's the whole flow for most reminders. Everything else is one chip away and stays out of sight until you need it.

  • Live reading. The day and time pills fill in as you type, and a sparkle shows they came from your words.
  • Repeats understood. “every day”, “weekly”, “every Monday”, “monthly” set the repeat and leave the title clean.
  • Quick moves. +1 hour, Tonight and Tomorrow in one tap; the time turns pink if it's already passed.
  • Extras on demand. Repeat, High priority, Team, Tag and Note — one opens at a time.

Your day

What's next, and one tap to finish it

Home opens on the one thing that matters now. Done and Snooze sit right on it — no opening, no scrolling. Plan groups the rest by how often it happens.

  • Next up with a live countdown, and an overdue card that never gets stuck.
  • Snooze in one tap: 5, 15, 30 minutes or an hour — the same everywhere in the app.
  • Done from the notification itself: Done, 15m or Later without opening the app.
  • Home-screen widget that shows what's next and updates live.

Teams

After 1, 2, 3 or 5 snoozes in a row the team is told — or never. Owners set it once for everyone.

👋

🌙

Quiet hours

Team pushes stay silent during your quiet hours — unless the team allows urgent reminders to break through.

Hear it, say “done”

Made for parents and grandparents

Reminders can be read aloud when they arrive — in English or Hindi — and answered by voice. With the app open, say “haan, ho gaya” and it's marked done, and whoever sent it hears back. “Not yet” and “nahi” are never mistaken for a yes.

  • Easy-listening mode speaks slower and lower, says the name first, and repeats once.
  • Voice quick add — tap Speak on Home and say the reminder.
  • Urgent can wake — medication reminders can ring through quiet hours if the family allows it.

Under the hood

Built for the moment the network isn't there

A reminder app that only works online isn't a reminder app. These are the parts that make it hold.

⚡

Exact alarms

Android's AlarmManager fires on the second, through Doze, and re-registers itself after a reboot. SynClock asks once, in plain words, and re-arms every alarm the moment you allow it.

📴

Offline-first

Everything is stored on the phone first (Hive) and synced when you're back online. An edit or a Done on one phone moves or cancels the alarm on the others too.

🧠

On-device parsing

Everyday phrasings are read locally in microseconds. Only the genuinely ambiguous ones go to Gemini, with per-user daily limits and caching.

🔔

Actions in the shade

Done, 15 minutes and Later work straight from the notification, even when the app is closed — handled in a background isolate.

☁️

Own backend

NestJS on Oracle Cloud with SQLite/libSQL, rotating JWT sessions, rate limits and push through Firebase Cloud Messaging. Repeating reminders keep going even when Done is tapped from the notification.

🧩

Widget & deep links

A live home-screen widget, and synclock:// links that open the exact reminder — never a blank duplicate.

Privacy

Your reminders are yours

No ads and nothing sold. SynClock keeps only what it needs to ring your phone and your family's, and tells you exactly what that is.

  • Delete your account in the app — your reminders, devices, AI usage and sign-in records go with it; shared team reminders stay with the team, with your name removed.
  • Encrypted in transit over HTTPS to SynClock's own server, and your sign-in is kept in Android's secure Keystore.
  • Your choice on analytics — turn off usage and crash reports in Settings.
  • Clear data-safety labels — what is collected, why, and how to remove it, in the privacy policy.
